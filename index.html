Injury prevention

Built around your Achilles and IT band. Tap a routine to open it.

Before every run5 min
  • Leg swings: forward/back and side to side, 10 each leg
  • Ankle rocks: knee over toes against a wall, 10 each side
  • Walking lunges: 10 total
  • Glute bridges: 10, to wake up the glutes
  • High knees + butt kicks: 20 m each, then start easy

Keep it moving. Save static stretching for after, not before.

After every run5 min
  • Calf stretch, straight knee: 30 sec each side
  • Calf stretch, bent knee: 30 sec each side (hits the Achilles/soleus)
  • Kneeling hip flexor: 30 sec each side
  • Figure-4 glute stretch: 30 sec each side
  • Hamstring stretch: 30 sec each side

Optional foam roller: glutes, outer hip, quads, calves. Skip rolling directly on the IT band. It doesn't loosen it and can irritate it.

Strength routine3× a week · 15 min
  • Eccentric heel drops off a step: 2 × 15 straight knee + 2 × 15 bent knee. Rise on two feet, lower slowly on one. Your main Achilles protection.
  • Clamshells with band: 2 × 15 each side
  • Lateral band walks: 2 × 15 steps each way
  • Glute bridges: 2 × 15. Move to single-leg bridges (2 × 10 each) once easy.
  • Side plank: 2 × 30 sec each side
  • Single-leg Romanian deadlift: 2 × 10 each side, bodyweight is fine

Recommended days are tagged in the plan: Tue and Thu after your run, plus Sun. That keeps hard days hard and easy days easy, and never the day before the long run. Taper week drops to two sessions, race week none. Track it with the three dots on each week.

Warning signsread once
  • Achilles: stiff for the first few steps in the morning, or tender when you pinch it. Swap the next run for rest and keep doing heel drops.
  • IT band: sharp outer-knee pain that starts at the same mile every run. Stop the run and walk home; don't push through it.
  • Pain above 3/10, or pain that gets worse as you run: stop. Two days off now beats two weeks off later.
  • Pain that hasn't improved after a week: see a physical therapist or your doctor.

Fueling

Every long run is a rehearsal for race day. Never try anything new on race day. Long-run days in the plan show how many gels to bring.

Up to 6 miunder an hour
  • During: nothing needed. Water if you're thirsty.
  • Before: a normal meal 2–3 hours out, or a banana or toast 30–60 min before.
7–10 mi60–90 min
  • During: optional single gel around 45 min. Good runs to test which gels sit well.
  • Fluids: carry water if it's warm. Denver's dry air dehydrates you faster than you notice.
  • Before: 30–60 g of carbs 1–2 hours out (bagel, oatmeal, banana).
11–15 mi1.5–2.25 hr
  • During: 40–60 g of carbs an hour. First gel at 30 min, then one every 30 min.
  • Fluids: 16–24 oz an hour, by thirst. Add electrolytes (300–600 mg sodium an hour).
  • Before: 80–100 g of carbs 2–3 hours out, such as a bagel with honey and a banana.
  • After: carbs plus 20–30 g of protein within an hour.
16–20 mi2.25–3 hr
  • During: build toward 60–90 g of carbs an hour, a gel every 25–30 min. Your gut adapts with practice, so push it a little more each long run.
  • Fluids & salt: same as above. Plan a route past a water fountain or stash a bottle.
  • Night before: a carb-heavy dinner. Eat race-morning breakfast exactly as you plan to on race day.
  • After: carbs plus protein within an hour, then a real meal.
Race day26.2 mi
  • Thu–Sat: carb load. Make carbs most of every meal (rice, pasta, bread, potatoes) and ease off fiber and fat. Expect to gain a couple of pounds of water. That's fuel.
  • Breakfast: 3 hours before the 6:30 start (about 3:30 AM), 100–150 g of carbs, the same breakfast you've rehearsed.
  • 15 min before the start: one gel and a few sips of water.
  • During: a gel every 30 min, about 7 total. Carry your own gels and don't count on on-course nutrition. Take water at most aid stations.
  • Caffeine: a caffeinated gel after mile 18 helps, but only if you've tried it on a long run.
